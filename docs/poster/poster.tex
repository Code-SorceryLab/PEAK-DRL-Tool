\begin{pblock}{Balance needs a thousand playthroughs. Humans give you ten.}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Level balance is tuned by playtesting: slow, costly, and \hi{not repeatable}. Move one
      saw, and last week's testers cannot be re-run.
    \item Deep-RL test agents work in industry~[1], but they need GPUs, reward shaping and
      per-game tuning: too expensive \emph{per level edit}.
    \item Simple agents can map difficulty~[2], and GAs rival deep RL~[3]. Our GA probe reproduces
      the failure-mode taxonomy of our earlier PPO version at about \hi{1/100 of the compute}.
    \item \textbf{Our goal: a regression test for game feel.} A frozen, cheap, deterministic
      playtester, so that a change in the metrics isolates the change in the level.
  \end{itemize}
\end{pblock}

\begin{pblock}{PEAK: play, measure, edit, repeat}
  \centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.25\linewidth,y=1cm,
      box/.style={draw=peakink,line width=1.2pt,rounded corners=2mm,fill=paper,align=center,
        text width=0.19\linewidth,minimum height=3.1cm,font=\small},
      outer/.style={box,fill=peakrose},
      arr/.style={-{Latex[length=5mm,width=4mm]},line width=1.6pt,draw=peakink},
      lab/.style={font=\footnotesize,text=body!80}]
    \node[box]   (lvl) at (0.5,0) {\textbf{ASCII level}\\+ YAML config};
    \node[box]   (eng) at (1.5,0) {\textbf{Game engine}\\deterministic};
    \node[box]   (sen) at (2.5,0) {\textbf{Sensors}\\14 floats};
    \node[box,draw=peakred,line width=2pt] (net) at (3.5,0) {\textbf{Neural net}\\14--16--3, tanh};
    \node[outer] (rep) at (0.5,-7.3) {\textbf{Report}\\win rate, deaths, routes};
    \node[outer] (prb) at (2.0,-7.3) {\textbf{Balance probe}\\levels $\times$ 3 seeds $\times$ 3 personas};
    \node[outer] (ga)  at (3.5,-7.3) {\textbf{GA}\\pop 10, elite 4};
    \draw[arr] (lvl)--(eng); \draw[arr] (eng)--(sen); \draw[arr] (sen)--(net);
    \draw[arr] (net.north) to[out=150,in=30] node[lab,above]{move, jump} (eng.north);
    \draw[arr] (eng.south) -- ++(0,-1.7) -| node[lab,pos=0.25,above]{fitness = progress + 5000 on a win}
      ([xshift=-1.6cm]ga.north);
    \draw[arr] ([xshift=1.6cm]ga.north) -- node[lab,right,pos=0.3]{next gen} ([xshift=1.6cm]net.south);
    \draw[arr] (ga)--(prb); \draw[arr] (prb)--(rep);
    \draw[arr,dashed,draw=peakred] (rep.north) -- node[lab,right]{edit, re-probe} (lvl.south);
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \vspace{3mm}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Agent:} fixed-topology MLP, 14\,$\to$\,16\,$\to$\,3 (\hi{291 weights}). No gradients, no
      replay buffer, no reward shaping, no GPU.
    \item \textbf{Evolution:} population 10, elite 4, tournament $k{=}5$, uniform crossover 0.7,
      Gaussian mutation (rate 0.15, $\sigma$ 0.15), annealed $\times$0.5 after a level's first win.
    \item \textbf{Probe:} a fresh population per level $\times$ seed; reports win rate $\pm$ 95\,\% CI,
      generations-to-first-win, death causes, death heat-map and routes.
    \item \textbf{One seed} drives initialisation, mutation and every game step: two runs are
      \hi{bit-identical}.
    \item \textbf{Cost:} 5,000+ steps per second on a laptop. A Mario probe (2 levels $\times$ 3 seeds
      $\times$ 40 generations) takes about \hi{six CPU-minutes}.
  \end{itemize}
\end{pblock}

\begin{pblock}{Personas: who is the level hard for?}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lXX@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Persona} & \textbf{Capability} & \textbf{Objective}\\
    \midrule
    Novice      & walk; senses refresh every 3rd frame & reach the goal\\
    Experienced & walk; full reactions                 & reach the goal\\
    Speedrunner & sprint; full reactions               & goal + 25 fitness per second left\\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
  \capt{A persona is a capability + objective profile, not a reward function~[4]. The win-rate gap
    between personas on one level is its skill-expression signal.}
\end{pblock}

\begin{pblock}{The probe in the design loop: Bomberman}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A draft level was reported unsolvable, one rung after a trivial one: its bricks
      \hi{sealed every route}. A reachability test now pins the intent of each level.
    \item The dominant death on every level was the agent's own bomb (55--97\,\%). Fuse 2.5\,s
      $\to$ 4\,s: level 6 went from 0/3 to 2/3 seeds.
    \item A range-1 bomb killed a wandering enemy in \hi{0 of 200} trials. Enemy speed $\times$0.7
      gave level 9 its first wins (0/3 $\to$ 2/3).
    \item Every change was A/B-probed on identical seeds: measured, not guessed.
  \end{itemize}
\end{pblock}

\begin{pblock}{Result 1: levels rank themselves}
  \fig{fig_difficulty}
  \vspace{3mm}
  \begin{itemize}

\begin{pblock}{Result 3: one knob at a time}
  \begin{minipage}[c]{0.56\linewidth}\fig{fig_knobs}\end{minipage}\hfill
  \begin{minipage}[c]{0.41\linewidth}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item 22 variants $\times$ 6 sweeps. Each knob moves to its literature bound~[5,\,6], paired on
        identical level $\times$ seed cells.
      \item \hi{One change wins everywhere:} anneal mutation $\times$0.5 after the first win. It is
        now the default.
      \item Population 100 is worse in 6 of 6, at ten times the compute.
      \item Elite 6, $\sigma$ 0.02 and no crossover each hurt a Meat Boy persona significantly.
      \item Winners go to \code{ga\_best.yaml} by strict majority vote.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{minipage}

\begin{pblock}{Limitations and next steps}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Reactive agents are a \emph{lower bound} on difficulty, not a model of a human. The probe
      is not yet validated against human play data.
    \item One-factor-at-a-time sweeps ignore interactions; three seeds give wide CIs on hard levels.
    \item Next: path and strategy-diversity metrics from logged trajectories, and risk/reward
      metrics from forced-collector agents.
  \end{itemize}
\end{pblock}
